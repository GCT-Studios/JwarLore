The city of Okyo sits perched on coastal cliffs at the tip of the island of Izu, looking out enviously across the Silver Strait at Ryu. Battered and bruised by the constant pounding it receives from the surrounding seas, it comes across as somewhat enduring in the face of such repeated beatings. It is located at the head of the only hospitable land for farming on Izu, yet what it lacks in refinement and niceties it makes up for in defensive qualities. Sharp cliffs on three sides and a steep southern approach from the lower lying arable lands make it quite a fortress.  
 
Thin soil and monsoon rains brought, it is thought, by Mireu and Imoogi make farming difficult on Izu and the indigenious people are a hardy bunch because of it. Life has always been tough here but when the Shiho placed the island under the stewardship of the Ito clan, things presumably went from bad to worse.  
 
Life is not all doom and gloom in Okyo though, one thing I adore and I’m sure visitors will appreciate is the sheer diversity of the architecture here. Whilst all the buildings share a common feature of being built small, due to the solid bedrock making only the shallowest of foundations feasible, that is where the similarities end. Over the years Okyo has not only been home to many ruling clans but has also been home to immigrants from across the Empire and beyond, each bringing their own style with them. There is a riot of colour to be seen, if not a little faded and slightly jaded from the relentless sea damage. Why one clan or people has not remained dominant for an extended period has many reasons, an obvious one being that those clans that show promise in such a difficult region often vie for a smaller yet more prestigious holding on Jwar or a return to the mainland. Much more interesting a reason, whilst surely being absolute nonsense, is that the island holds a deep seated curse, a series of natural yet freak occurrences breaking the will of one clan or another, plagues, floods and once in a lifetime storms, all seemingly targeting the occupants of Okyo, if that’s what you will believe. That said, they do seem to have had quite their fair share, although of the plague I do have a theory based on my travels that it is the rats that inevitably travel for free on the trading junks that are to blame. I will have to gain some further evidence before I write more on this subject though. Others though remain convinced by the old tales that tell of an ancient evil, as old as the Kami, that lies sleeping beneath the island, waiting.  
 
As well as varied buildings you will also find a huge range of temples, shrines and Honden built to worship all kinds of Kami and family ancestors, some now long forgotten. And although the upkeep of much of Okyo is falling by the wayside, the people come together in a briiliant show of unity to maintain these holy places, no doubt finding reassurance that if they and their people move on someone will show their own such respect. There is even an example of what is apparently called a Church, its founders passed on but the odd far flung visitor still comes to genuflect before it, as I understand it they believe in just one, all powerful, Kami.  
 
The grandest of these weird and wonderful constructs is the Temple of Orochi. It has thrived under the guidance of the Ito Clan, it is claimed the cost of green marble has risen across the whole of the Jwar Isles as the Ito voraciously snap up any available to clad the walls of this snake Kami they worship so fervently. The Ito who have never been the wealthiest of clans are certainly investing their family’s wealth here, some of the emeralds are astounding and the rubies that adorn the eyes of Orochi are the size of a man’s fist. Presumably the Minimoto’s coffers have benefitted greatly.  

The Temple of Orochi in all its new found grandeur is however symptomatic of the whole of Okyo. Whilst the wealth of the Ito is put on show at every opportunity the underlying poverty of the people is apparent without really looking for it. This eclectic mixture of peoples so warm and charming, so inviting despite their hardships, the very humanity is in stark contrast to the welcome received from the Snake clan’s authorities; corruption is rife and a moon in the right place goes a long way here on Izu. This, for me, gives Okyo a darker feel, knowing the very people who are here to serve and protect, ranging from those that walk the streets to those that hold court, can be bought so easily is unnerving to say the least.  

One can also see it in the workshops here on Okyo. Whilst the fronts have artisans sat carefully almost artistically plying their trade in a show for the public that could almost rival those in the artisans district of Ryu. Grease your way into one though with a copper moon or two and you will see not a workshop but a sweathouse, row upon row of workers lifelessly and repetitively producing the same item over and over again, day after day.  

Much of this can be forgotten if not completely forgiven when you venture into the wild and raucous great market, the sights and sounds of Okyo build to a crescendo here. You may not believe it till you visit Okyo but there are men here that play a merry tune making their deadly snake companions dance for the crowds. In fact a dancing snake is a reason to visit any city in my humble opinion. After this entertainment one must be adventurous and try some of the delightful foods on offer. The ever increasing waves of immigrants to Okyo has deposited many layers to not only the culture but the cuisine on offer as well. The great market is a melting pot of all of them, even the simplest of street stalls produce dishes for impoverished workers, fishermen and even samurai eating side by side like Emperors.  

Many foreign traders sell their wares amongst the (dare I say it) inferior products made here on Okyo and it is brilliant just wandering from stall to stall listening to the banter and gossip between merchants and the locals, trading information of distant shores and more local rumours respectively. My last visit was dominated with talk of a group of mad monks living in the jungles of Shimogamo. Dependent on who you believe they came seeking a place to establish a new temple and were driven insane by the curse of Izu. Now it is popularly believed they live a cannibalistic lifestyle whilst rampaging through the wilderness. These rumours I mention because their truth has been given a certain credibility as the Ito clan have offered a bounty on their heads. Strange only in that they do not normally dip into their own coffers to ensure the safety of the people entrusted to them.
