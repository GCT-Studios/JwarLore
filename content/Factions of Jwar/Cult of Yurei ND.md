---
source: New Dawn rulebook
title: Cult of Yurei
---
The Cult of Yurei is by its very nature and structure a secretive organisation. Distinct cells of the Cult operate independently. The members of some of these cells have received such levels of indoctrination that they believe they are pursuing their own goals, and in truth they often are. Those that find themselves attracted to the Cult are already on a path that leads them in dark harmony with the powers of Yurei and their goals have similar resonance. What they lack however is the understanding of how their actions combine to further the ultimate goal. Some within the Cult may still even have enough of a spark of humanity left to be horrified to learn of their true cause; a world in which hope is only tolerated because it allows for true despair, where life is only present to feel pain and misery. Despair, pain and misery, these are the feelings and emotions that feed Yurei and make it stronger.  

It would appear that the Cult also feeds on the weakness and anguish of others more directly. One can only expect to be presented with the Yurei Bargain when one is at his life’s lowest point. This is where a man’s moral compass can be spun off target, when the path our ancestors set for us is pulled from under our feet and we head off blindly along a darker road. Despair turned to determination, depression becoming a desire for vengeance. The weak are given strength, the ignored the power to be heard. All of these ‘Bargains’ grant individuals a tantalising opportunity to fulfil their darkest desires, but at what cost? Individuals like Kato, the puppet master, may find that by the time they have achieved their own goals, they no longer know who or what they are... a twisted tool of Yurei. Not all are turned by the Bargain however, some make choices in their life that have led them at every step towards Yurei. They embrace the opportunity offered by the Cult without conscience or regret.  

The Cult’s strength is hard to measure for the clans, little information is extracted from those captured, even when the most persuasive techniques are applied. Another problem for the clans in identifying the threat of the Cult was naming it. Even though for the last few years all the clans have seen a rise in the activities of these seemingly separate groups, no one had been able to make a link between them due to their ability to operate so autonomously. Such incidents are now thought to include the loss of the great city of Sagoya as well as the plague that ravaged the people of eastern Jwar in 288 SK.  

The emergence of the Savage Wave threat and the slow break down of the Prefecture has also meant that the clans have recently had to focus on the tangible and conclusive threat of what they can see and fight, buying the Cult time to fester and grow in the darkest corners of society, and the Cult is definitely growing and so is the power of Yurei. As the old barriers crumble, the monks are spread thin, their numbers a fraction of those needed to stem the tide of the returning Oni. The death, destruction and ever increasing imbalance are all feeding the strength of Yurei. The clans are though finally sitting up and taking note, whispers in the courts fret about the growth of the Cult, some of those that discuss this believe that its members sell their soul to a dark power, a force of evil personified known only as Yurei. Others believe that Yurei may only be a concept, an ideal that allows the Cult’s members to justify their heinous crimes.  

As the Cult expands it is drawing attention and has created a deadly enemy for itself in the form of the Savage Wave. When the ‘puppet master’ Kato took the great Oni, Araka, for a slave, he created a fear the Oni have never felt before. A glorious death loses its meaning if you are brought back from the dead a mindless puppet. The dark powers of the Cult, necromancy and mind control go
against everything this warrior race believe in, leaving the two factions mortal enemies.  

One thing that is certain is that the overarching aim of Yurei means that no one is safe from the threat of the Cult. Every man casts a shadow; some are darker and deeper than others.
