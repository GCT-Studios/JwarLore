The storm is a warning, he thought.

[[Grand Master Ekusa|Master Ekusa]] watched as large black clouds grew balefully on the horizon, pregnant with rain and violence, and with the wind lashing their backs, spurring them on, he knew that no one could outrun them. The storm would catch all in its path.

Master Ekusa felt the chill more than most, not only because of his age but also because his powers made him more attuned to what countless others were not. The storm is a warning, he thought again. Most definitely. Of that, the ancient master had no doubt.

He stood at the temple’s doors staring out over snow-covered mountains and river carved valleys where the last days of warmth cooled as soon as the sun sank and the shadows grew. His gnarled hands found his beads instinctively, and he began walking the worn pieces of wood through his fingers one prayer at a time until completion; then, he started again.

The storm is a warning; he thought once more—a prophecy of what will become, an omen of where our gaze should lie, and for all our supposed foresight and intuition, many will not heed the augury.

For too long, we have simply seen and not seen further than required of us. We’ve become deaf and blind to the real strife in Jwar, the conflict that lies beyond mere oceans and mountainous borders, city-states and Empires with ever-shifting alliances. The danger lies just over the curious curve of our horizon in realms others never even knew existed. Master Ekusa knew what he had to do; he just hoped there was time enough to do it. He turned his head to summon the others before the wind robbed the words from his mouth, and for the first time in the temple’s existence, he shut and barred the doors.
