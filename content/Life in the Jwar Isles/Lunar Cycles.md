In the Jwar Isles time is now measured by the cycle of Lady Moon, each year being divided into twelve cycles. Jwarians are a very superstitious people and great emphasis is put on the different cycles. Take for instance the ninth cycle of the year; the cycle of the Bleeding Moon, so named due to astrological events conspiring to paint the moon red during this the most inauspicious of cycles. This cycle has, since time began, been attributed with bad luck and misfortune, nine is therefore considered to be the unluckiest of numbers. The extent to which this can be seen is that traditionally families would abandon babies born during these times to the elements rather than have a child born in the cycle of the Bleeding Moon. This practice has since been banned by the third Emperor of the Golden Sun dynasty, although rumours still persist that in the remotest regions of the Jwar Isles the laws are broken, better to face the wrath of an Emperor than the Kami!  
All is not doom and gloom in the Jwar Isles though, the opposite view is taken of the cycle of the Golden Harvest. A cycle blessed with good weather, when bountiful crops are harvested and the everyday life of a working family is that little bit better, named so to please the Kami of Luck and Harvest. The number seven is held in high esteem in the Jwar Isles due to this link. Many have tried to employ the luck of the number seven on the Mah Jong and ‘37’ tables in Jima. Many have failed.  
The naming of some of the cycles is not as clear cut, take the cycle of the Weeping Sky. The obvious reason is that it is an incredibly wet season when the heavens open and the thirsty ground and rivers drink again. The Sune- Kudansu truly earns its name during the cycle of the Weeping Sky. The cycle however has a different meaning for some, it marks the end of the campaign season in time of war. The weeping not referring to rain but the tears of mourning from wives for their husbands, mothers for their sons and children for their fathers when it is clear which men will not be returning home.  
Each lunar cycle lasts twenty eight days, the approaching cycle is ushered in by a new moon and this day is traditionally marked with a special meal shared between family members, with the exception of the arrival of the Bleeding Moon. This makes the Empire of the Golden Sun’s year three hundred and thirty six days.
