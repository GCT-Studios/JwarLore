Wanting to see more than the steppes which had raised him, Batu travelled the Empire earning money through his labour or, reluctantly, as a mercenary. Whatever earned him enough to feed himself and Temujin; whatever allowed him to keep moving.  

After some years of travel Batu found himself forced, through lack of other work, to join with a group of bandits. They weren't great fighters, but their numbers and their well-concealed cowardice largely led them to avoid bloodshed through intimidation. This suited Batu. He felt enough shame simply robbing their victims, let alone murdering them for what few valuables they carried.

So it happened, one day deep in autumn, that this band of outlaws made the mistake of ambushing a lone traveller. His choice to travel alone may have given the group pause had it not been clear to all that their intended victim was at the less fortunate end of recent merriment.  

By the time half of them had had the chance to flee, the other half were already dead or dying.  

The swordsman turned his attention to Batu, who found himself trapped. Riding Temujin for a gap between the trees, he was all too aware of his former quarry closing fast. Having seen so many arrows struck from the air by the mountain of a man who now charged towards him, he waited for the stillness of his saddle in mid-stride, and loosed his own. His skill proved enough; the arrow found its mark. The man halted his advance, his geta pinned to the ground. Batu guided his horse through the gap, urging it to a gallop before consciousness shattered from a blow to the back of his skull.

When he awoke, Batu found himself propped up against a tree. He could smell sake as strongly as if he had been doused in it. Night had fallen and a campfire burned not far from his feet. As he saw the man who had built it, he registered the headache which had woken him.
