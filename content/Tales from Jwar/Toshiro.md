---
title: Toshiro?
author: Jason Enos
---
She awoke to the sound of her one-of-a-kind, porcelain tea service crashing to the ground. Had someone entered her tent in the night? She bolted upright and felt for the tanto beneath her pillow. There was no Tanto. There was no pillow. There was no bed.  

Her eyes were already adjusted to the twilight, she was not in her tent. The sound she heard was not her tea service but a macaque biting into some fruit. She didn’t know much about the animals but she had never seen such a monkey in this part of the Empire. Perhaps it's a pet?  
Who cares about the monkey? Zhongwei thought angrily. In battle such slowness would mean death.  

“If you’re here to fight, you best stand up, little lady” a voice behind her rang out in the night. Not loud but clear and every syllable perfectly enunciated.  

As she rose and spun Zhongwei realised she was fully armoured and drew her sword.  
“What are you doing here? Who are you? How did you get me here?” she shouted.  

The figure, only visible as armour and weapons silhouetted against the moonlight, sighed.  
“Well, that's gone and done it… here they come.”  

Before Zhongwei could ask more, they were already under attack.  
A dozen or more bleached-bone walking skeletons advanced on them.  
Just the two of them would soon be overwhelmed.  Still, the best way to survive a battle was to win it, she began her work.  
Her companion seemed well skilled and carried a variety of weapons suited to different uses.  He pushed them back with great hammers, smashed bones with heavy maces and attacked with two weapons at once to deliver vicious damage.  
Zhongwei was about to be out flanked when she felt a presence protecting her on the right.
She was surprised to see a great silverback, holding a skeleton by the skull, repeatedly smashing the head into the ground until it stopped moving.  
On their other flank, next to the man, was a baboon protecting his back in the same way. The man didn’t show much on his face but he was both relieved and exasperated by the creature’s presence. She saw macaques, several of them harassing the osseous horde on the way in, slowing them so the line could deal with them piecemeal. Strange figures, with faces and limbs but made of the Water were slowing the advance on the other side.  
She had no time to wonder what was going on here.  
“The best way to survive a battle was to win it” she said aloud as if to reinforce the lesson she learned long ago.  

The bellowing gorilla was eventually overcome by the press on the flank, lacerated and bleeding from many sword, spear and bite wounds.  
Zhongwei concentrated on killing the foe in front of her, if it died fast, perhaps she could deal with those closing in on her but this one wore armour and seemed trained in combat. Any moment she would be attacked by more enemies…  
Any moment.  
Any moment…  

The attack never came. As she finally finished the dead thing in front of her she looked to the flank and saw the gashadokuro. Two of them, each with three arrows neatly fired into their chests, lying motionless on the floor. Zhongwei saw a fox’s tail disappear into the bamboo and she swore there was a bow there.  
It seemed they had won.  

Zhongwei turned to the other figure, she hated to do this, preferring to keep this quiet as long as possible, she produced a beautifully written Imperial Proclamation, covered in wax seals, ribbons and signatures.  
“You will tell me what is happening here!”  Zhongwei held the title of authority out so the samurai could read it. He didn’t even turn to face her.  

“Looks like we won…  First time?”  

Incredulous that this samurai was not even looking at her while questioning her battle experience, Zhongwei grabbed his shoulder and spun him round to reveal a worn, weathered face, eyes squinting but alert, looking in every direction.

“First time?  I am a veteran of the Golden Legion.  I have fought in dozens of battles!”  

“I don’t doubt it.”  He said.  “But first time for… this?”  That gave her pause for thought.  
Eventually his eyes fell on the papers she was holding.  
“Imperial eh?  That's new.  Maybe they want to tell someone who can do something about all this?”  

“They who?  How did you get me here?  Where am I?”  

“Don’t look at me. I only work here.”  

Seeing that this man was in the same predicament she was, Zhongwei relaxed.  She turned around to examine this place, this battlefield.  
“Who has done this to us?”  

“Heh, you may be friends with the Emperor but now you’ve really attracted the notice of those in high places!”  

Zhongwei  spun back round to face the samurai.  “I’m not frie…” the sentence cut off in her throat.as she fell backwards and ended up sat on her bed.  
A dream?  
How bizarre, fighting alongside gorillas, foxes and a strange man.  Like no other dream she’s ever had.  
Perhaps it is an ill portent, she should get up and check the security of the camp. Maybe they should review the campaign strategy. Should she confer with General Hagane? Many thoughts flashed through her mind. Suddenly she froze, looking at the table set up for her meals. She found her tea service smashed on the floor, surrounded by Macaque footprints.  

“It's happening again, just like before.”  
“Not happening again, it's happening for the first time, just like we have foreseen”  
“What can we do?”  
“We do what we will do and have already done.  There is no choice for us.”  
Master Koju laid back on her large cushion on the floor to rest; the other figure left her cell, dismissed.
