It was on the road that the tengu king met the Toad. It was, of course, raining. [[Sojobo]] did not need to look at the heavens to know that Raijin watched him, and waited, however impatiently. A mortal rice farmer stood stoically beside the tengu monarch.  Sojobo found the peasant praying at a roadside shrine.  He lost his rice fields, washed away by the Tsunami. He then had to flee his farm house as thousands of spiders used it to escape the waters and it was covered in webs.  

The villager was holding aloft an umbrella. The device was utterly doomed in the face of Raijin’s all-encompassing wrath. Its spokes were barely attached to the fabric. When the lightning flashed it seemed to illuminate the sky with such fury that the fabric did nothing, all that could be seen were the spokes against the white. Nonetheless, the gesture held its own significance.  

There was nothing of note on the road except a small statue shrine that took the shape of a fox. Sojobo patted its head affectionately as he watched Toad meander towards him. Toad walked with confidence, a surety that the world conformed to Toad and not the other way around. That Toad walked at all was a sign of mutual respect between the tengu king and Toad. The three pointedly ignored Raijin glowering from above, for it was not the season of revenge.  

Toad stopped a suitably polite distance away, allowing Sojobo the courtesy of observation. Sojobo extended Toad the same. To the mortal it would seem as if they had both bowed respectfully to the other. The tengu monarch appeared as a long-nosed monk, while Toad was a rotund man with bowed legs. The mortal could not perceive Raijin at all, only the consequences of his presence.  

“I extend greetings to you, Lord Toad, and implore you to rest a while at Inari’s shrine.”  

“I will hear your words, Sojobo.” Toad replied in a voice deep and throaty. He squatted down next to the fox statue. The statue seemed amused to observe such a meeting.  

“This humble king cannot help but notice that the heavens above my realm have become unbalanced.” Sojobo waved a hand towards the sky, again ignoring Raijin.  

Toad croaked, as close to a laugh as he could manage. He spread his arms wide, “It is not for you to dictate what becomes of the heavens my friend. Do you not enjoy the fruits of my cycle?”.  

“This Weeping Sky has been most bountiful indeed Lord Toad.” Sojobo replied courteously. It was not an untruth, but it was certainly not the complete truth. The peasant remained heroically stoic in the rain.  

“Wondrous is it not? Do you think Bat would offer such if we let him bring forth his Bleeding Moon? I think not!” Toad said indignantly.  

Sojobo knew Bat would be walking abroad soon. “I would argue that Bat’s Bleeding Moon brings balance after the great fortune you provide Lord Toad. That you have seen fit to descend from the heavens has been a great honour, but now the heavens lie vacant.”.  

“What is this need for balance Sojobo? My brothers and sisters have descended, each of them fools. They think their cycles greater than mine!”.  

It was a foolish thought to challenge Toad in his own season. Sojobo would have to choose his works carefully to avoid insult.  

“My lord, all things change, even us. You are not the first to believe their cycle to be superior, nor claim primacy at the height of your power.” Sojobo kept his tone carefully neutral, it was a subtle reminder and less subtle threat.  

“Mine is a cycle of birth, not ruin, Sojobo. Do not mortals not benefit from my rule? It was a cycle of destruction that brought us together, you and I, and all my siblings.”.  

Sojobo could remember those distant days, of blood and fire. They were not fond days to remember.  

“And together we made great things, Load Toad. I beseech you to return, to urge your brothers and sisters to go with you. The mortal realm cannot remain beholden to a single cycle. Already your cycle turns from bounty to calamity.” The tengu king implored.  

Toad made a disappointed croak, “I had hoped you would be above such nonsense Sojobo. The greatness of the Weeping Sky has only just begun. Now, leave the affairs of the heavens to your betters and go back to your mountains.”.  

“As you say Lord Toad. I benefit from your wisdom in this matter.”  

With that, Sojobo bowed to Toad. He had been dismissed. The tengu king trudged onward, confined to the ground his journey would be long. Sheer will sweeping up the miserably wet peasant in his wake, Sojobo continued to the end, towards the rising sun. If Toad would not listen to reason, then other, more desperate, measures would be taken.  

The forest was dark and oppressive. Lady Amaterasu spread no light to this place, and it was no comforting night under these trees. They had passed by the boundary posts, to an unsuspecting passerby, they might appear to be nothing more than ordinary signposts. However, the intricate carvings etched into their surfaces and webs draped across them reveal the hidden truth: These are the boundary markers of the prison of a Celestial.  

The peasant shuffled nervously closer to Sojobo as he could manage without seeming to intrude. The undergrowth clung to his clothes and he struggled to free himself. The more he spun and dragged, the more of the undergrowth he became caught in. It was a normal moment but in this strange twilight it was a moment of panic. The light of the peasant’s lantern was somehow diminished. It only served to reveal glittering eyes in the dark. Those eyes were many.  

All of Sojobo’s efforts had led here. To this foreboding and forsaken forest. Sojobo could feel the ancient seals and prayers stretched across the waterfall, holding their ancient prisoner at bay. The tengu monarch stood at their very edge and placed a hand on a seal so old it had petrified. He was greeted by a sensation akin to drowning as the prisoner brushed against the barrier closest to him.  

*Have you come to gloat, oh mighty king?* The question was both mocking and had weight. A veil for deeper meaning. Sojobo gave the spirit wary respect. They had really asked: *Why are you here?*  
Sojobo could not tell her. Kiyohara’s scroll writhed and curled in his hand. It was a hole in the world, pregnant with possibility but so very, very dangerous. He pondered for a moment that a mortal could have crafted such a thing, and what it meant to use it.  

“You ruled all of us once. Filled our stomachs with fear and called it food. Did you not think we would learn from this?” Sojobo shook his head.  

*You have come here, back to this place tengu king. You drove me from the heavens. I ruled. I made the celestial order. Not you mewling cowards. You come here a beggar.*  

“That is truth.” The very words he forced from his mouth grated against the mantle of kingship and Sojobo’s very essence. He considered the possibility that he may be permanently diminished. “I come before you a supplicant. Will you hear my plea?”.  

Only silence answered. Sojobo continued with his request.  

“Your brothers and sisters have come down from the heavens. They have forgotten the order the zodiac brings and their sacred charges. I ask that you return the stars to the sky and restore the celestial order.”  

There was a pressure building in the air. The glittering eyes seemed to edge closer. The peasant made a whimper, and huddled ever closer to Sojobo. Something that could be laughter came from the other side of the seal. It lasted a long time.  

*You would ask me to battle my kin for the same reason they did against me? You need only break your part of the seal, little bird, and I will tear them apart. Your precious balance would be restored.*  

“In that way lies only destruction. You would devour the world in your hunger. It would be an unending cycle of fear.”  

*Then why come here tengu king? You ask the impossible. Oh, I could crack their bones and suck on sweet marrow. Do you propose to bring each here to join me?*  

There was a simple logic to it. Each could be brought to heel and imprisoned here. This place was made for such things. Yet, the cage had many locks, and each lock had been crafted by the embodiment of a cycle. It would not take long for one of them to simply release their seal, undoing aeons of effort. If the prisoner did not eat them.  

“Would you allow yourself to be bound to a single task? Freedom to accomplish only this one thing and nothing else.”  

There was a long pause as the spirit considered his question. It was already contemplating ways to abuse the limits of the request. He would have done the same. It would not, could not, refuse the offer. What came next would be most delicate. A misstep would spell disaster for all.  

*I accept your generous offer.*  

“This is the realm of the physical. What form will you take to enter this world?”  

*Break the seal, that I may taste the physical and select a form. Break it now Sojobo, I will not be imprisoned again.*  

Anyone else, including the thing behind the seal, would have to contend with the will of twelve celestials to open it. Each having put a measure of their power, cunning and nature into it. But Sojobo was there when the thing was imprisoned, it was his royal seal that began the process. His mantle of Kingship eased the door open but a sliver.  

The prisoner stretched a tentative tendril of themself across the threshold, testing for treachery. This was the keystone in the plan. If Sojobo failed here, all would be lost. The once-prisoner touched creation. Even Sojobo could feel fear in her presence. He had to wait for the precise moment.  

Sojobo clenched his fist carefully behind his back. He took a half step away from the mortal peasant, pulling what spiritual protection he offered away from the mortal. The effect was immediate. All those glittering eyes inched closer. Wild panic began to fill the peasant. His recent experiences filled his mind, the shape of the umbrella like eight spindly legs, the undergrowth like being caught in webs, his home, the eyes, those eyes, many eyes…  

*SPIDER! Their fear of them is instinctive. They cannot even bear to look on this glorious form. And they kill Sojobo, they KILL!  Murder in many forms, each worse than the last.*  

The prisoner’s voice sounded ecstatic. She began to pour her essence from unreality into a new form. Like a crone or a hag, but with the legs and spinner of a spider. Her body still shifted as it found the right combination of old woman and spider to inspire the most terror in those beholding her.  

It was then Sojobo acted, while only a fraction of the prisoner's self was in her physical shell. Behind the petrified mortal, he unfurled the Last Scroll of Kiyohara.  

If Lord Toads constant rain and Raijins perpetual lightning storms were not enough, arcane winds blew from nowhere. Strange lights flashed and shone, rain fell sideways and boiled, the heavens were no longer above and the thousand hells no longer below, they each stood perpendicular to reality. The shifting form of the new spirit was engulfed in sorcerous energies.  

*What is this? Treachery? Kings must honour their bargains!*  

“The bargain will be honoured!” This proclamation would prevent any agent of destiny deciding he acted against his dharma.  

The villager saw the tengu monarch speak the blistering words of the scroll. The prisoner saw Sojobo manipulate the hole in reality. To Sojobo himself, he wrestled his infinite will upon the nothing in his hands and was confronted by two arachnid crones.  

They immediately lunged at each other. As with the Raijin’s daughter, the split selves would not abide the other’s existence. A slight lift from the ground, his divine right as monarch enforcing his will; Sojobo buffeted one of them back across the threshold to rejoin the greater part of the prisoner. From under his wing, Sojobo revealed the seal in his hands, opened but not broken. With it he closed the threshold once again. The prisoner thrashed against the boundary of the prison but reality bent against it, for the cage was sealed.  

The new creature outside stalked toward Sojobo and moved straight for a killing blow.  She froze in place just before committing her first murder. She strained against her own essence and could not complete the act.  

*No!*  

“We are in agreement, a pact is made.” Sojobo smiled. Such promises mean something to these beings and they are bound by them. “Killing me does not advance your one task.”.  

*You think you have won? You think I am defeated again?*  

She smiled, it looked odd. But then, Sojobo considered, it was her first ever, so he could forgive that.  

*This form suits me well. Spider lays many eggs, and has so many tricks and methods of murder. Bound perhaps, but I will become the Grandmother Spider, at the centre of the web. My sons and daughters and their sons and daughters are unbound, free to work my will across creation. My cycle will return.*  

“Then I take my leave.” Sojobo never had looked less dignified than this one moment, “Many will suffer greatly after tonight. I would ask that you not give in to base desires, but it is your nature and such pleading is beneath me.”  

Sojobo turned to leave.  He gave a quick nod, not to Grandmother but to the Prisoner, still in her cage.  The Prisoner returned the gesture, unnoticed by her twin. He regarded the peasant, his mind would never recover from exposure to the creatures full might. This was regrettable.  

He began another long walk, his work was far from done, but turned at the edge of [[Jorogumo's Hollow]] and addressed the fragment one last time.  

“Remember this well, ‘Grandmother’”, Sojobo put as much scorn as he could muster onto the word. “Birds eat spiders…”.
