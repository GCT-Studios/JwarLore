The Isle of Goto lies to the North of The Hook in the storm-lashed waters of the Frozen Sea and is home to a giant volcano that spears the watery horizon like a shark’s tooth.

The volcano is supposedly long dormant, but brave fishermen often tell of it rumbling, stirring the ocean, and trickling thick, black columns of smoke and steam into the fresh sea air.

Supposedly, a small group of fishermen came to shore upon its mysterious beaches during one of the frequent storms that plague that part of the sea.

They fled their boat, looking for shelter on the island as large bolts of fury-white lightning hammered the ground around them. One of the survivors later likened it to being a piece of metal under a Minimoto blacksmith’s hammer.

He also said that the sand on the beach is ink-black, thick, extremely coarse, and had spewed from the earth’s bowels long ago. There were no bushes nor blades of grass on those darkened dunes, he added, and the only sound of life was that of screeching seabirds darting through the storm.

The men dragged their fishing boat onto the shore then ran toward the sheer cliffs that vaulted high up through the mists and clouds, so obscured, they had no way of knowing where they ended. The storm was relentless, and it lashed the men with rain and battered them with large hailstones that ricocheted off the cliffs and covered the black sand in what looked like deep snowdrifts.

They stumbled across an ancient set of hand-carved steps that went from the beach high up into the slate-grey clouds that broiled above. None of them happened to be brave enough to climb it, so they continued along the cliff face until they found a small cave that looked to be above the high tide mark.

Careful exploration of the cave led them to journey through cracks and crevasses until they came to a vast chamber that echoed with its enormity. Countless lichens and fungi glowed brightly on the floor and walls, and the men thought for a moment that they gazed upon the stars in the night sky. A soft chanting from within the cave snapped them out of their fugue state, and they proceeded to go further into the chamber.

They came across a giant, stone hand and forearm that reached out of the ground holding a ring of worshippers on its palm. These robed disciples disappeared then reappeared seconds or minutes later as if nothing had happened. A large floating construct, or spirit, floated in the middle of these curious travellers with ripped and timeworn tendrils of cloak floating all around it as if it lived in water rather than the air. One fisherman remarked that the monks - for that was what he thought they must be moved sinuously, like a drop of black ink in calm, clear water. The spectre held aloft a strange lantern that emitted a deep, rich, purple light, bathing the worshippers in its uncanny hues as the figures flickered in and out of existence like tiny candle flames in the dark. Their chants sounded otherworldly, as if they were spoken backwards like the speaker inhaled the words rather than projecting them.

They spied black holes, glowing fiercely and looking like the sun would if it happened to be eaten by the moon; they appeared, then disappeared, then reappeared again in the palm of that giant, stone hand. The noises that escaped from those infernal portals sounded horrific, such screams of torture and pain that it made the fishermen shudder in abject terror and fear for their lives. The men fled the cavern leaving the strange monks and their unearthly practises behind, knowing that an unnatural blight was growing in the land.
