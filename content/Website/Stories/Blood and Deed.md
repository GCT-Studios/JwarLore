## Chapter 1
It was an unassuming clearing among the cherry orchards just off the dry, cracked road between Ryu and Eddo. The town of Kobe could almost be seen to the north, floating on the horizon like a scrim of winter snow. It was a strange place for a battle, the orchard, and an even stranger place for a family to pit its skills against one another. Madness.

Hiro knew this place well; he had led many ashigaru on patrols through the region, keeping the Dragon’s peace, enforcing the law and maintaining the safety of this vital trade route. If not done regularly, bandits sprung up like weeds, robbing goods and kidnapping the local headmen for coin. By all accounts, Hiro should have let the younger samurai lead these patrols to gain experience, but he always chose to lead the way; he felt grounded by spending time with the locals as their worries and troubles were his to share and solve if possible.

The land was as familiar to Hiro as a lover’s face. Otakebi village is just over the rise, he thought, taking a deep swig of water from his waterskin. The headman’s wife makes us dumplings whenever we pass through; it’s her way of thanking us for saving her husband’s life two seasons ago. A gust of wind brought cherry blossoms spiralling down from the branches to the ground below. The day was hot, and the cool air was welcome, and to Hiro, the falling blossoms reminded him of winter’s first snowfall.

A short distance away below the marshalled men, a lake sparkled and shimmered, catching the sunlight, making it dance on its silken surface. Hiro liked to rest here while on patrol; he enjoyed the view and the sense of calm it brought to him. While not particularly observant in religious duties, Hiro always left an offering - something small yet significant - for the kami that dwelt in the lake and the hollow, as well as the breathtaking cherry trees. He loved this place, yet doubts clouded his mind like the storm clouds he saw marshalling on the horizon.

“Is this it?” he said softly, “Is this the place where I write my death note as I lay bleeding on the field of battle?” He threw small pebbles into the long grass letting his thoughts and fears run amok in his mind’s eye like a herd of wild horses on the plains of Ataka. Why has it come to this? Fate pitting us against one another. He sighed aloud before beginning to pace, his hand tight around the handle of his weapon, Kusanagi. The legends said it could burn its foes with dragon fire, yet Hiro had never seen it. More likely it was a story told to make the wielder seem more than just a man. Now it was nought but a sword wielded by a fool who did not want to use it against his own family.

“Should I allow others to die needlessly so that I may be Daimyo?” he said to a fallen blossom on the back of his hand. He picked it up, careful not to damage such frail beauty.
He heard the unmistakable sounds of an army on the move behind him and knew at once what he should do. Holding up a hand to stay Himitsu and his men, Hiro walked forward to meet the other force. Even now, he could not think of it as an enemy army; that particular thought struck him as odd.
If he survived, he would write a haiku about it. If.

Hagane was ready for battle. She felt like she was always ready for battle these days.
“What is this place?” she asked the man to her right. He didn’t know, and upon being told he’d find out, she told him not to bother; she didn’t care. She had a mind only for the terrain that could help or hinder her troops and the strategic location of the battlefield.
She looked down the length of the army assembled around her. Nobles mostly, not the heart and soul of the Takashi army, no, those she saw across the field alongside Hiro. They loved him, but more importantly, they’d die for him. Everyone loved him, the poor, the villagers. Even the garrison at Ryu, her own men, had refused to march on Hiro. Though they may need to be disciplined, their defiance showed their loyalty to her cousin. It made her proud of what Hiro had become. At each village they passed the headman asked Hagane to pass on their condolences to Hiro, daring to address someone vastly higher in status.
Hiro had massed ranks of Ashigaru from Eddo, wearing the heavy plate and carrying their distinctive naginata instead of yari. Hagane hated the weapon, the clan elders had tried to force her to learn it when she was younger but her superlative skill with the blade soon silenced such nonsense.
That was odd. Why had she thought of her youth just now? And before that of the journey here and those village headsmen. She was usually so focussed when it came to battle or dueling. Why was her mind wandering to the past now?

Something in the enemy lines caught her eye and ceased her reverie. One man advancing into the space between the armies. One man alone. This was more like the Hiro of yesteryear! Bold! Decisive! She walked forward, mirroring her cousin’s gait, ordering her men to hold the line unless otherwise instructed. There were questions Hagane meant to ask, and before their ancestors, she’d have her answers!

Two figures walked from the massed ranks of their army toward their destiny. Both would fight for their clan in their own way. But first both would know why. Why had their cousin betrayed them? Why had they betrayed their clan? Not just the clan but the ideals of the dragon. Both small figures walked forward. They would ask, and they would have answers.

Hiro could see a small number of Hagane’s retainers advancing toward him, weapons out, horses moving quickly. They would not do so without reason. An ambush? Never! Hagane would not even consider any foe, even Hiro, a match for her blades. She would not bring others. If she wished to kill him she would do it herself.
“Hagane wishes to kill me?” he thought, it just seemed so wrong. Then Hiro understood why her cavalry advanced, he looked behind and saw Himitsu advancing with a small number of his retainers. Why had the man disobeyed? This was to be the one chance for him to talk to Hagane before the bloodshed.
He would offer her the Seat! He would follow her as Daimyo. The Dragon Clan would stand. Now with this seeming aggression, Himitsu may have started a battle that would end the clan.

Hagane saw Hiro’s men begin to advance. An Ambush? Hiro would never…
But then that’s the point here isn’t it, she thought. “Hiro had been acting unlike himself... and unlike a Daimyo.” Hagane wished she could talk to him, discover the reasons behind his seeming cowardice but that chance was lost, now there would be blades and blood. The Dragon is called to battle.

With two Dragon armies facing off against each other for the first time in the clan’s history, Hiro considered the available options. If there was a battle here today, the dragon clan would fall. The casualties on both sides would decimate the Dragon army and the distrust between family members fighting on opposing sides may never be overcome. He knew only one way to stop it with honour on all sides. Hiro drew no weapons. Kusanagi at his side, he kept striding forward, towards Hagane.

Hagane saw how the battle would start, now inevitable. Once the small groups began to fight, both armies would be forced to advance in support or be left outmanoeuvred. Hiro was still walking directly toward her. Perhaps he thought as she did? Maybe just one Takashi had to fall? Was Hiro really coming to kill her? Just weeks ago she would have laughed at the very idea. Again, something scratched in the back of her mind. This was not the act of a coward. The only charge laid at Hiro’s feet was cowardice. That he had hidden from possible assassins. He had refused to leave Eddo, even for his father’s funeral. He had refused to lead the clan against the minor clans, rising up, looking for an advantage. But now Hiro calmly walked to face not only Hagane, but an entire Dragon army alone? Hiro’s actions now did not add up to the reports she had received. But then, Hiro did miss his father’s funeral….

As they came within fighting distance, Hiro realised he was too late, Takashi Himitsu and those men he had brought had somehow caught up to them and Hagane’s followers raced to reach them.
Hagane too realised the fight would not be stopped. Her men came in riding as hard as they could, they would not hear her now over the sound of the hoofbeats. She saw Himitsu, sword drawn and odd little tanto in his off-hand seeming ready to fight her cavalry but moving in behind Hiro.

Hiro and Hagane’s eyes met across the field. In that instant the realisation came to both. Hiro knew what Hagane was about to do and Hagane looked into Hiro’s eyes and knew the one course of action to end this with just one death.

Hagane charged forward, swords both drawn. Rage and fury channeled into her strike. Ten thousand hours of practice led to this one sword form, there was no error, no counter stroke would suffice. Years of non-stop teaching from three blademasters went into creating this one perfect attack.
Hiro kept his eyes on Hagane, he did not draw his sword, he did not move to defend, he stood and beheld Hagane as she charged across the battlefield, faster than any opponent would have thought possible.

The last thing Hiro thought before Hagane’s sword found its home was how perfect her form was, each footstep in place, eyes focussed on her target, torso twisting to lend power to the blow, if Hiro must die today, he knew he was blessed simply to have seen this one stroke. Here was a true inheritor of The Dragon.

Then it all ended, Hagane’s first sword cut into body armour and sliced open flesh mere seconds before her second blade found its way under the Takashi armour helm sending the severed head to the ground some meters away. Hiro hadn’t moved a muscle. He had just watched Hagane. Hiro did not even draw his sword.

“That is true courage” Hagane thought. She was not sure her own bravery would ever match what Hiro just did. Hagane was convinced she just saw The Dragon’s spirit made manifest in a man.

## Chapter 2
Hiro turned. He saw a mask drop to the ground from one of Himitsu’s hands and a poisoned dagger in the other. He saw Himitsu’s head, still in his helmet but far from the body. The leftovers of Hagane’s attack made it seem like ritual suicide but there was no honour in Himitsu. Everything buzzed through Hiro’s mind, quickly. Hiro was still looking into Hagane’s eyes and she was looking back, breathing hard and still holding her swords in the end of her follow through. Himitsu’s plan, whatever his reason for this treachery, had almost succeeded.
He didn’t count on one thing. Hiro and Hagane were cousins. Of similar age, they grew up together. They knew each other better than they knew themselves.
When Akira was killed, it was Hagane who came to comfort Hiro in the night, while he wept for his brother’s death, knowing she would never tell another soul of it in case it was seen as weakness. It was Hiro who stood with Hagane when she told Uncle Mako she wished to study at another clan’s Koryu. Hagane would not have managed to stand before the old man and say the words without Hiro’s courage there to support her. A lifetime of experience together, a love one can only have for family who have always been there. Hours, days, years of playing together, training together, fighting together.

Himitsu may have seen to it that they only had seconds before the fighting started.
It took just one glance. In the seconds where they first came face to face, Hagane saw the courage in Hiro’s eyes and knew Himitsu had lied to her. Falsely accusing Hiro of cowardice. Then she saw Himitsu and understood the whole treasonous plan.
Hiro saw her eyes widen when she saw Himitsu, he knew instantly she did not expect him to be there. Then he thought of the disobeyed order that brought these men to him, forcing Hagane’s men to do the same. Hiro saw her fury and instantly understood everything.

Hagane must have assumed Himitsu would be with her own army. He had fed her some false information that led her here. He had been the only one in contact with Hagane and Hiro. He was the only one who could have done this. Hiro Knew Hagane could kill Himitsu, but if he were wrong he would be dead. While some saw courage in his refusal to defend or even take arms against his cousin, Hiro simply trusted her.
Hagane knew if she removed the traitor, she could live with any outcome, if she were wrong she would leave herself open to Hiro’s attack but she could die with her duty fulfilled. The Clan would stand.

Hagane immediately prostrated herself before Hiro. The other samurai on both sides stopped, not sure how to respond.
“My Daimyo,” She said. “I apologize for the actions I took in your absence. If you wish it, I will end my life.” Before Hiro could respond, she added “The clan must remain strong. Any who followed me here will fall in line after my death.”
Hiro shook his head. “You acted in my stead when I was unable to act, as any loyal retainer would.” Hiro smiled. “You did the Clan proud and acted with honour, you will continue to do so when you take on Uncle Mako’s role as my Warmaster.”

Shouts and sounds of marching men drowned out any possible response.
The two armies were still fast approaching, unsure as to what was happening, there could still be casualties if they were not stopped.
Hiro walked towards Hagane’s army. He drew Kusanagi from its scabbard.

Two armies, as one, stopped dead. Every man and woman; commoner, noble and priest alike dropped to their knees and bowed to the floor.

Hida’s sword had burst into Dragon Flame as Hiro held it above his head.

## Epilogue
Himitsu’s body was left to rot. No kind of burial was allowed for a traitor. His name would be stricken from the Clan and his family would be made peasants if they did not kill themselves. But his body was simply left, no care was taken over a traitor’s afterlife. At midnight it rose, mask firmly affixed to his severed head and walked into the sagoya swamps.
