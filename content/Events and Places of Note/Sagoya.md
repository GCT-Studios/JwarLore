The ‘City of the Dead’ sits at the foot of the Ro-Kan Mountains. As its name suggests it is no longer inhabited and the locals avoid it with a fervour based on old superstitions and a fair bit of common sense.  

Following the Shiho’s final stand in the Dragon Wars at the swamps of Sagoya, two years later in the year 286 SK during the cycle of the Bleeding Moon, an inauspicious month at the best of times, the first sightings of Kairai, dead men walking, were recorded. Peasants that fished the swamps were mainly ignored, their reports of the Shiho back from the dead to wreak havoc on those that turned their backs on them put down to the partaking of too much sake and not enough work.  

As occurrences became increasingly common place, a ground swell of opinion amongst the populace suggested they were cursed and were being punished by the Kami. How this gossip seeded so quickly and widespread was unknown at the time, but the Cult of Yurei is now
suspected. Under this dark cloud of doubt, families ebbed away to forge lives elsewhere.  

Before long this once proud city found itself semi-deserted, trade slowly ceased as rumours spread across the Jwar Isles as well as there being increasingly no one to trade with. As resulting food shortages began to affect more than just the lowest of Sagoya the underlying panic turned into mass hysteria, neighbours stepped on one another in their efforts to flee as the mob took control. Some stayed and relished in this new found freedom, the shackles of civility and Bushido finally broken.  

The great Sagoya with its four finely built city walls fell not to an army but from the rotten core of its society.
