source: New Dawn rulebook
title: Trade City of Ataka
---
Ataka’s prominence at a major crossroad in central Jwar along the Iron Road has allowed it to flourish like the wild flowers in the sea of savannah surrounding it. But before I begin describing the trade city of Ataka to you, it is as important for me as it is for yourself to remember that familiarity breeds contempt, even those less well travelled than myself will have passed through Ataka en route to some other place and therefore have some experience of it. I have been more times than I could recount as I have journeyed across the lands. I will try to fight any jaded feelings I have, whilst I endeavour to capture the thoughts and feelings this city stirred in my younger self.  
 
Ataka sits astride the Plains of Ataka as proud, tall and confidently as the Horse Lords that dominate Ataka’s political landscape straddle their own mounts. From afar one is struck by the great structural majesty of Ataka, its profile rises from the surrounding flatlands at its western edge to tower into the sky at its eastern border, framed against the sky like a lop sided triangle of which the academics at the blue libraries of ‘Sen no Machi’ will no doubt have specifically categorised and named, in their pursuit of knowledge. The contrast this creates between the quiet, low lying and flowing grasses of nature and the feats of man leave me feeling conflicted and torn, so different and yet so special in their own ways.  
 
The city accounts for some of the tallest buildings in the Jwar Isles and this in itself is no small feat, combined with the fact that the nearest forest is leagues and leagues away makes it somewhat spectacular. These predominantly timber structures are quite odd when you consider the nature of the people that continue to build them. A people happier in the outdoors than the home, in the saddle than on foot and most importantly at ground level than the heights their many floored properties so adequately provide. The social system so ever present throughout Jwar is very much evident in the homes of Ataka. Those that can afford such grandiose buildings live with their family members at the bottom, as you rise upwards the status of the inhabitants declines until in some homes the highest reaches hold no one at all! The wealth of the families that build them is the crux you see. The height of one’s building has somehow, and now the reason must surely be lost to the annals of time, become a way to measure status, symbolising the power and dominance of the family within.  
 
This strange practice (which I find absurd) is also maintained and perhaps perpetuated at the few guesthouses on the eastern side of town that have more than one floor. I myself heartily recommend the largest of these ‘Sakura House’ a well-run affair with excellent Sake, the ridiculous fact being that the top floor is available for almost free if one is able to assure the owners that you are certain about your choice. The less than modest room is to be ignored; room being the wrong word as there is probably not even enough room to swing a Bakemono. For a couple of copper crescent moons, the four small windows do however provide an almost panoramic view of simply splendid sights; plains that seemingly never end even from the giddy heights at which you will find yourself, and if one is lucky or privileged enough to time their visit during the cycle of the Burning Sun, then you will have an unrivalled seat to one of the greatest shows in the Empire; the ‘Horse Parade’. The samurai and retainers of every Horse Lord assemble to show off their riding skills and martial prowess whilst mounted. Of course, down on the ground the lords and their families sip ice cold Sake in the shade, but from up here you can truly appreciate the skill with which these riders maintain their formations, track their arrows as they buzz into the air making their dummy targets dance on the end of their ropes. Each Horse Lord hoping to watch his force out-do those of his neighbours.  
 
Less of an occasion I suppose but you can at most times of year also see a whole host of traders travelling the roads that cross at Ataka; trappers out of Fuwa to the south west bring mules loaded with the skins of a range of animals, long tree trunks are pulled down from the ‘Twilight Woods’ to the north east. Along the Iron Road come oxen with strained muscles drawing carts packed with heavy metals from the Iron Mountains to the north west. These raw products are processed and most often can be seen leaving Ataka in the possession of rich merchants from the south east, sure to make a stop at Ryu before moving on again across the Jwar Isles. The merchants these days are, it is obvious to note as I think about it, more heavily protected than at any time I can remember. Bandit attacks are led by the disbanded remnants of the Shiho clan; so swift, so ferocious that the Prefecture patrols are often, from what I hear, too little too late. This is not a concern for any travellers such as myself or likely any of my readers, the attacks are growing in local legend as targeted against others rather than the common man, I merely pass on what I hear though and these are not my opinions.  
 
Ataka is a hive of activity, as I previously intimated a wide range of goods from across Jwar find their way here to be chopped, smelted, tanned and refined before trundling along again on their path to the trade markets of Jwar and then on to artisans who will once again shape and form them before sending them onwards on their journey many leagues from where they began life. This industrial activity is almost certainly the primary driver of the cities architectural peculiarities, although I don’t think blame for the fixation for tall buildings can fall at its feet. Over time the various hovels that popped up to work on the raw products initially clumped together in the centre of Ataka when it was a young city in the dark ages, all eager to be the first to get their hands on the products passing down the great roads. These rapidly grew into great factories belching out smoke from fires and smells that as you can imagine weren’t the least bit desirable to the Horse Lords that watched over the city as it worked and grew. The prevailing easterly winds meant that any gagging odours and cloying smoke blew west over the inhabitants there and so any self-respecting samurai did the appropriate thing and moved east to get a breath of fresh air, and then at some point the arms race to build the tallest house must have become common place.  
 
This most overt competition, for what can be more obvious than a ten storey building, between the rulers of this great city is also the biggest indicator as to why they have never posed a threat to the various other major clans that have held dominance over Jwar. Although friendly, their rivalries have prevented them from ever presenting a united front to the world, despite the Council of the Plains. Also their loyalty to the ruling clan and the empire has never been questioned. This has in the long run served them well, the city remains un-sacked even though it possesses no wall to mark its limit and deter would be aggressors. But what general in the known world would dare march his army across the Plains of Ataka and face a cavalry charge from the Horse Lords of the Horse clan? Certainly any that had seen Ataka’s entire host assembled at the ‘Horse Parade’ would take pause for thought. An ashigaru would no doubt, even when surrounded by his own army, feel as lonely and exposed out there on those rolling plains as a man on his own at sea. As waves of arrows wash across him, his Yari would provide as much comfort and likely as much use as a single oar.
