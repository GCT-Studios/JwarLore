source: Stories of Yoshimori

The Sun was already low set only a few
moments and it would disappear behind
the soft foothills of the Iron mountains.
Ikeda pulled his Kimono tighter.
Although the days were already pretty
warm for spring the evenings could still
be very chilly especially so close to the
river.
He was strolling along the landing stage
of the northern river docks. Dressed in
common robes he looked like more of a
dockworker or fisherman. He perfectly
blended in.
His eyes were locked onto a small junk
where a few dock workers were still at
work binding together bulky tree trunks
and letting them float and readying
them for transport on the water. One of
the men with bare upper body and dirty
Hakama was shouting orders. Ikeda
took a deep breath and approached the
Man. He turned to Ikeda and fixed him
with a severe look.
“Whatch ya’ want? Don’t ya’ see we are
busy!” He rudely bellowed in Ikeda’s
direction.
“The sea is calm tonight. The tide is full,
the moon lies fair.” Ikeda answered
calmly.
The man looked at him in silence. Then
he broke into laughter.
“Ey! Ikeda don’t stand around there like
a fool. Come aboard!” he waved him
onto the ship.
“Thank’s Sho-san!”. Ikeda gave a slight
bow although Sho didn’t even bother
with this kind of etiquette. Ikeda was
used to it.
Sho brought two small barrels to sit on
and a crate which served as table. He
wiped it clean with one hand although
Ikeda doubted it would be any cleaner
after this procedure, still he sat down.
He put two sake cups on the crate
which he wiped before with his dirty
Hakama.
“For ya’ I brought the good stuff” he
winked at him and poured the Sake
hastily into the two cups.
Ikeda tried to hide a disgusted look and
took a polite sip from the offered cup. It
tasted unexpectedly well. He wished he
could have had the time to question
Sho where he could have acquired
such a fine sake.
“Ya don’t want to waste ma`precious
time so whats up?”
Ikeda took another sip and answered in
a calm voice.
“It’s no secret that the Ito are short on
people and needed to hire Ronin to
keep an eye on the iron mine as well as
the rice fields. It also seems like their
motivation is…let’s say compromised.”
“And why should I care?” he emptied
out the cup with one sip.
“See, I need someone who keeps the
Ronin busy, I don’t care how it’s done,
and you could keep whatever you “find”,
I heard they discovered another rich
silver lode recently.”
Sho thoughtfully scratched his chin.
“And whats in for ya’?”
“Let’s say if it goes well, I would look
forward to continue our business.”
The Kaizoku crossed his arms before
his chest and examined the trader with
a thoughtful look.
He nodded.
“I think this man got himself a big catch!
Geonbae!” He laughed and raised his
sake cup cheerfully.
Ikeda couldn’t help but proudly smile on
his cunning and raised his cup in turn.
