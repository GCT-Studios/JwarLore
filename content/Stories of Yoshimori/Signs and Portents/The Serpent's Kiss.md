---
source: Stories of Yoshimori
---
Fubatsu had never been into the
residence of the Ito clan before. That he
was doing so now while escorted by two
guards did not bode well. He could feel
a slickness building up in the small of
his back as he nervously glanced
about. Neither of the guards had
spoken a word or offered any hint
behind the reasons for his summons.
His gaze wandered around the
wondrous garden. Most of the exotic
fauna was planted on top of cuboid
stones, half sunk into the wet ground.
The long stalks stretching far down to
reach the nourishing ground. Colourful
leaves of Bonsai trees and flowers
fascinated not only the human visitors,
but many types of birds and insects.
The serenity of the garden was only a
minor reprieve for Fubatsu's nervous
mind.
Fubatsu swallowed down the nervous
thought that the reason could be any
number of things. He had been too
greedy. The Ito must have decided that
he had overstepped, even beyond their
lax standards. The urge to bolt and run
grew with each step through the
corridors.
When they entered the garden at the
eye of the complex, he could feel that
his mouth was dry, having somehow
diverted the moisture to his palms.
Fubatsu rubbed his palms against the
hem of his kimono nervously. The sight
that greeted him did nothing to allay his
fears.
A girl has prostrated herself before a
monster, while an ashigaru knelt beside
her, his head bowed down and his gaze
making a study of his own knees. The
monster towered over both, long and
sinuous as it swayed ever so slightly.
The upper body was that of a woman,
and Fubatsu felt the blood drain from
his face when he realised her hair was
not hair at all, but a writhing mass of
snakes. Instead of legs, below the
waist, the monster was a serpent.
He had not realised that he had stopped
walking until one of his guards prodded
him sharply in the back, motioning
Fubatsu forward. It took another prod to
get his legs to cooperate, and he stood
before the monster. He forgot to bow.
“Is this the one?” the monster asked,
her voice silken and soft. The snakes of
her hair hissed. Fubatsu heard the
sound, but his mind failed to turn it into
words.
The prostrate girl lifted her head from
the ground, just high enough to look at
Fubatsu. Tears had left rivers down her
cheeks. A chill went down his spine and
he felt beads of sweat form on his brow.
He knew the girl, and she knew his
greed.
“Yes Mizuki-sama. That is he.”
“How fortunate that Genkei is not here.
The judgement is mine.” the monster-
Mizuki purred, fixing Fubatsu with a
languid stare.
Her eyes were a brilliant emerald, he
had never seen their like before. His
breath caught as the world began to
shrink, smaller and smaller until all he
could see were two pinpricks of
emerald.
Fubatsu blinked. And the world
returned. The monster-Mizuki still towered over him, but now there were
two large baskets placed on the ground
beside her. She had turned her
attention to the girl.
“You have served us well, you shall join
Orochi.” monster-Mizuki said to the girl,
her voice gentle and consoling.
Whatever reward was, the girl did not
want it and her voice broke as she
pleaded.
“Please mistress, I have been loyal. I-”
“Hush child, there is no shame in
embracing the comfort of Orochi’s
coils.” the monster swayed lower to the
girl, lifting her face gently. The girl’s
face went slack as they locked gazes
and Fubatsu heard the girl’s sudden
intake of breath. He knew that sound.
The monster cradled the girl’s head,
lowering her to the ground with a lover’s
care. In one of the monster’s hands was
a long knife, deadly and sharp. Fubatsu
could see the crimson stain spread to
the gravel beneath the girl. He’d heard
rumours about the Ito. They had all
proven true. Monsters and murderers.
Monster-Mizuki returned her attention
to Fubatsu and the ashigaru, who had
remained unmoved, some species of
human-shaped statue. Perhaps the
ashigaru was made of sterner stuff or
had seen monsters already. Or perhaps
fear had frozen him in the same way it
had done to Fubatsu.
“You have both failed me. That silver
would have served me well. You may
die now or face the serpent’s
judgement.” Her snakes hissed in
excitement as she motioned to the two
baskets, “Make your choice.”
The kneeling ashigaru raised his head
just enough to look at the baskets and
began to roll up one sleeve. “I am loyal.
I choose the serpent.”
Fubatsu stared in amazement as the
ashigaru thrust his arm all the way to
the elbow into the basket. The
movement was greeted by angry
hissing, and the ashigaru allowed pain
to cross his face several times. Then he
began to shake, and Fubatsu saw white
froth begin to bubble out of the
ashigaru’s mouth.
The ashigaru fell to the ground and
began to convulse as the monster
turned to face Fubatsu. The spasms
were so severe that it looked like there
were snakes moving beneath the
ashigaru’s skin.
“Make your choice. The serpent does
not kill all those who receive the kiss,
some are blessed,” the monster
motioned at the writhing ashigaru, scale
patterns appearing on his skin, “some
are killed. And for some, nothing at all.”
Fubatsu looked at the ashigaru, whose
face had begun to change colour, and
back at the basket. The monster smiled
at him. It knew. Fubatsu began to roll up
his sleeve, his hand shaking.
